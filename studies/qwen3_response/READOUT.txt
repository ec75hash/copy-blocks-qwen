QWEN3-32B: SAME-INDEX MLP NEURONS 24 LAYERS APART
Exploratory readout, October 27, 2026.

Question
Does neuron i at layer L+24 respond to the same passages as neuron i at
layer L, more often than different-index neurons do?

What held up
In this sample, same-index partners usually resemble one another more
than the random controls do. Across all 288 pairs, 264 have a higher
token-response correlation than their control mean. In 185/288 (64.2%),
the same-index partner ranks first among the partner and 100 controls.
Top-100 passage lists also overlap more on average for the true partners.

What did not hold up uniformly
The resemblance is usually modest. The median activation correlation is
0.0555; being the best of mostly near-zero controls does not mean the two
neurons respond nearly identically. The median pair shares four of its
top 100 positive windows and four of its top 100 negative windows.
Among the strongest ten windows, the median overlap is zero for both signs.
Some pairs have much stronger overlap, while others resemble controls.

What was measured
The official Qwen/Qwen3-32B-GGUF Q8_0 model ran locally on 10,000 fixed
256-token WikiText-103 windows. Every channel has 2,560,000 token
observations. Text was fed directly, without a chat template, BOS token,
or generated answers; context reset between windows. One separate window
was used for instrumentation and is excluded from these results.

The sample covers original zero-based decoder layers 8-31 paired with
32-55, with 12 selected neurons in each of the 24 layer pairs. Each source
layer has four samples from each index segment: A=0-8191, B=8192-17407,
C=17408-25599. Because these segments have different sizes, the equally
sampled aggregate is not a population-weighted estimate of all neurons.
Auxiliary MTP and vision layers are excluded.

Controls are 100 random different-index neurons per target layer, shared
by its 12 source neurons and excluding all 12 true targets. They were not
matched to the source neuron's response frequency or strength. Thus
"unrelated" means different-index random controls in this experiment;
their semantic relationship was not established.

The main response is the signed MLP scalar SiLU(gate) * value, before
the down projection. Gate before SiLU and value were measured separately
as well. This is neither the gate alone nor the neuron's resulting vector
contribution after the down projection.

How the two comparisons work
1. Overall similarity: Pearson correlation between the two response
   curves at the same token positions, over all windows. It measures
   how much they rise and fall together. A near-zero result means weak
   linear similarity; it does not establish an absence of all relationships.
2. Strong-passage overlap: rank each window by its largest positive
   response, or by the magnitude of its most negative response, and count
   shared windows in two top-K lists. The two peaks may occur on different
   words. Positive and negative lists are separate.

(284), and 1.955 vs 1.071 for gate-/value- (287). This records which
measurements differ without treating negative-gate events as missing.

Concrete examples, with selection rules
These cases were selected after looking at the numerical results. They
illustrate the range; they are not an independent test of neuron meaning.
PASSAGE_EXAMPLES.txt gives marked peak tokens, contexts, signs and values.
examples.json also retains all five strongest windows per member.

L30/L54, neuron 11460: highest positive top-100 overlap, 76 shared windows
versus 8.12 for controls; token correlation 0.0857. Four equally strongest
source windows begin "In mathematics , the", with the same response at
token 3, " the". A shared Pythagorean-theorem window peaks there in L30
and at token 171, " he", in L54. A shared Ramanujan window peaks at the
"ore" piece of "theorems" in L30 and a whitespace token before "900" in
L54. Sharing a passage therefore does not establish the same trigger word
or a single mathematical concept. Repeated initial context is visible.

L14/L38, neuron 7563: highest negative top-100 overlap, with the higher
correlation breaking a tie: 54 shared windows versus 1.25 controls;
correlation 0.3918. In one shared Bach-cantata window, the source peak is
the "burg" part of Schwarzburg and the deeper peak is the "stadt" part
of Rudolstadt. In another shared window, the peaks are the "lich" part
of Jülich and the "ves" part of Cleves. These are literal observations
about the saved text, not a validated subject or name-detector label.

L20/L44, neuron 7369: nearest the median final-activation correlation,
r=0.0546; its partner ranks third of 101. It shares one positive top-100
window (controls 0.919) and three negative windows (controls 0.848).
Source positive peaks include " submarine" and " island"; deeper peaks
include " lit", " the" and " divides". The displayed strongest examples
do not establish a common meaning for this pair.

L23/L47, neuron 5461: lowest signed correlation, r=-0.0607 and rank 101.
It shares one positive window (controls 2.04) and two negative windows
(controls 0.939). Source positive peaks include words in opening titles;
deeper peaks occur elsewhere. Positive correspondence is not uniform in
this sample. This case does not prove that the two neurons have unrelated
roles; no opposite-sign feature-equivalence analysis was performed here.

Corpus correction and remaining scope
The collector's title parser sometimes mistook equation lines for article
headings. The 10,000 saved window texts are distinct, but they cannot be
described as 10,000 independent articles. Source rows and token IDs are
preserved. CORPUS_LABEL_CORRECTION.json records exact offending rows and
the parser condition. The retained capture inputs preserve the original sampling description;
CORPUS_LABEL_CORRECTION.json supersedes that independent-article description
for this run. Metadata path redactions are listed in ../../docs/AMENDMENTS.json.
This is a real preparation defect, not merely a spelling correction:
some windows start after false headings, and one underlying article can
contribute more than one selected window. It does not change the saved
response measurements, but article-level sampling was not achieved.

A post-hoc check uses the 512 preselected windows with full paired-channel
curves. Median correlations are 0.0494 using all 256 positions, 0.0488
after excluding positions 0-7, and 0.0500 after excluding positions 0-31.
That sample's median does not disappear when initial response positions
are omitted. Inputs and their prefixes remain unchanged, and raw controls
were not retained: this is neither a causal prefix test nor a fresh
paired-versus-control comparison. It does not resolve repeated wording or
within-article dependence. See analysis/prefix_sensitivity.csv.

The controls and windows are not independent replications. No predictions,
p-values, confidence intervals or definitive same-feature verdict are
assigned. Results describe this Q8 checkpoint, corpus and neuron sample;
direct equivalence to the earlier BF16 weight pin is not asserted. Nor do
these response comparisons identify a neuron's causal contribution or
explain why the checkpoint has repeated weight structure.

Open question
If you understood the earlier neuron from its strongest passages, how
much of that explanation would you expect to carry to the same-index
neuron 24 layers deeper? What would you measure next to decide whether
the overlap represents a shared feature, especially when token peaks
differ and typical overall similarity is small?
